Circular To Linear Movement

Making & Prototyping | 3D Print

PDF Summary PowerPoint Slides

Overview

Honestly, this was the toughest project so far in 2025 Design Challanges. Every small part and every bigger element went through at least 10 different versions before anything even started to make sense. The whole setup is basically a case with a long rod inside, and on both sides I built a pair of rotary levers. These levers are linked to each other to create the circular motion, and then connected to the sliding piece so that the rotation turns into linear movement.

One of the biggest problems was that the lever holder blocked the larger lever, so the mechanism literally couldn’t rotate. After that I redesigned the joints, used “holey” screws and normal ones to let the pieces move smoother and actually go the full 360°. Since my chosen quality was making a quick and dirty prototype, I kept everything simple, cheap and rough, but still sturdy enough to run for more than 10 minutes without failing.

Reflection

This really pushed me the most out of all the projects so far. I had to redesign the same parts over and over again, and the first two full attempts simply didn’t work. I didn’t understand the mechanic at all in the beginning. But the more I tested, watched examples, asked for help and actually paid attention to what was happening, the more things clicked.

By the end, I finally understood how to control the circular-to-linear motion and how to avoid blocking my own mechanism. It became easier once the logic sank in, and honestly this ended up being the project I learned the most from. Even though it’s a quick and dirty prototype, it did exactly what it needed to do, and building it taught me more than any of the smoother, easier assignments.
