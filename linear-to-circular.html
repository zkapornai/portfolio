Linear to Circular

A quick-and-dirty mechanical prototype linking rotary levers to a slider, converting circular motion into linear movement.

  • Mechanical design
  • 3D modelling
  • 3D printing
  • Prototyping

01-02 / First ideas · Motion study

Sketch the movement

This was the toughest project so far in the 2025 Design Challenges. Every small part and larger element went through at least ten versions before the mechanism began to make sense. I started by sketching how a rod could move and how a rotary lever might drive a slider, before working out the mechanism in 3D.

Follow the rod's path

Sketching the rod movement helped define how rotation could be transferred to a part moving along a straight path.

03-04 / Early geometry · Connections

Try a first shape

The first digital shape was a starting point for exploring the fundamental parts and how they might fit together.

Work out how the parts meet

The squares and their connections began to define a case for the mechanism and the joints needed for movement.

05-06 / Core parts · Assembly view

Build the fundamental assembly

The setup takes shape around a case, a long rod and a moving square. Each part needed to work together as the rotary levers drove the sliding piece.

Inspect the mechanism in 3D

Viewing the parts together from different angles helped make their spacing and interaction clearer before fabrication.

07-08 / Linked rods · Check clearances

Connect both sides

A pair of rotary levers on both sides are linked to create circular motion, then connected to the slider to turn that rotation into linear movement.

Look from another angle

Another viewpoint helped check how the levers, rods and supports sat in relation to each other across the assembly.

09-10 / Joint detail · Fabrication

Connect the rods and holder

The cylindrical connector joins the rods to their holder. The joints needed revision so the lever could rotate without being blocked.

Prepare the parts for printing

Once the assembly had a workable form, I prepared its components for 3D printing so they could be tested physically.

11-12 / Printing · Fabricate

Build the first parts

The first printed components included supporting material as the complex parts took shape layer by layer.

Let the print run

The printing stage turned the digital components into parts that could be assembled, handled and evaluated.

13-14 / Printing progress · Printed parts

Wait for the parts to finish

As the print neared completion, the mechanism's individual elements became tangible and ready for assembly.

Inspect the result

Looking over the print from another perspective helped check the parts before testing the movement.

15-16 / First print complete · Review

Bring the parts off the printer

The completed print made it possible to move from checking the geometry on screen to testing a physical mechanism.

Check the components from all sides

A further view of the finished print helped assess how the pieces could fit together in the complete mechanism.

17-18 / First fit · Troubleshoot

Test the printed pieces

The first test pieces exposed issues that were hard to predict in the digital model, making hands-on assembly essential.

Remove the rotation block

The lever holder blocked the larger lever, preventing the mechanism from rotating. I redesigned the joints and used holey and standard screws to improve movement and allow a full 360° turn.

19-20 / Iterate · Improve the joints

Test the rods again

Another assembly test still showed issues. The first two full attempts did not work, so the parts went through repeated redesign and adjustment before the movement made sense.

Give the rods room to move

Printed screws were added between the rods to improve the connections and help the mechanism move more smoothly.

21-22 / Final assembly · Components

Put the complete mechanism together

After many versions of each part, the final assembly brought the case, rods, rotary levers and sliding element together.

Gather what makes it work

The parts shown together make clear how the separate printed pieces and connections form the working assembly.

23-24 / Working prototype · Simplify the view

Test circular-to-linear movement

The final mechanism performs the conversion it was designed for: linked levers create circular motion and drive the slider linearly. Kept simple, cheap and rough, it was still sturdy enough to run for more than ten minutes without failing.

Show one side clearly

I made the mechanism again with a single side rod to make its movement easier to see and understand.

25-25 / Reflection

Understand the mechanism through iteration

I did not understand the mechanics at the beginning, and the first two complete attempts simply did not work. Testing, looking at examples, asking for help and paying attention to the movement helped the logic click. By the end I understood how to control the circular-to-linear motion and avoid blocking the mechanism. This quick-and-dirty prototype did what it needed to do, and taught me more than the smoother, easier assignments.
