Paper Prototype — MacBook Charger

Recreating my everyday MacBook charger with paper, experimenting with materials, layered construction and hands-on techniques.

  • Physical Construction
  • Laser Cutter and Engraver

01 / Reference · Materials

Start with the everyday object

I chose my MacBook charger because it was an object I already used every day. I measured the original and explored how cardboard could be shaped to recreate its proportions. This was my first time building a paper model, so understanding the material was part of the challenge.

Material exploration

I learned that different paper stocks serve different purposes. Stiffer cardboard helped create the structure, while thinner paper was easier to shape around the edges.

02 / Physical Construction

Build the layered structure

I cut and stacked seven cardboard pieces to create the main body. Keeping the layers aligned and stable was harder than expected, especially when joining them together.

Refine the outer form

After assembling the core, I covered its exposed edges to create a cleaner surface. The process showed me how much precision and material choice affect the final shape.

03 / Laser Cutter · Details

Add the smaller details

I used the laser cutter to create the logo and prepare the smaller charging components. This let me work on the details separately before adding them to the main body.

Combining laser-cut parts with hand-built paper construction helped me understand how different techniques can work together in one prototype.

04 / Assembly · Fit Check

Bring the parts together

I joined the separate sections and refined the connector area. Small adjustments made a noticeable difference to how closely the model matched the original.

Checking the USB-C opening against the connector helped me see the importance of testing the fit instead of relying on measurements alone.

05 / Final Model · Reflection

Learn by making

I'm genuinely proud of how close the final model came to the original, especially compared with my first attempts.

Insights & Learnings

The hardest parts were keeping the internal supports stable and recreating the rounded edges. I learned that making an object is more than getting its appearance right. Material choice, structure and precision all affect the result.

Measuring, adjusting, fixing and trying again taught me more than simply following a plan. I began to understand the difference between imagining an object and actually constructing it.

Further Development

I would improve the dimensional accuracy, make the edges smoother and redesign the internal supports for better stability. I would also test different paper-based construction methods before assembling the final model, so I could identify problems earlier and achieve a closer match.
