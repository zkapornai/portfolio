Faux Feels

Faux Feels explores how digital surfaces can feel rough or uneven without using physical haptic devices. The project uses changes in cursor movement to create the impression of touch.

Pseudo-Haptics

Pseudo-haptics creates touch-like sensations through visual feedback. For example, slowing down or changing the movement of a cursor can make a digital surface feel heavier, rougher, or more resistant.

The prototype uses a hexagonal surface where cursor movement changes during interaction. The aim is to create a subtle, uneven feeling through delays and changes in movement, rather than obvious animations.

4. Demonstration and Reflection

The video shows how the cursor responds when moving across the surface. The main challenge is making the movement feel like resistance rather than a visual glitch. Further development could explore different levels of roughness and test how users perceive the effect.
